# Design Changes — Why They Help Customers Stick Around and Buy

Plain-English notes on the 6 style changes made for Problem 10: what changed, and why it should make a customer more likely to stay on the site and actually buy something.

## 1. Paired a serif font with a sans-serif font

Before, every single piece of text on the site — headings, prices, descriptions, buttons — used the same old-fashioned serif font. Now the big headings and the logo keep that classic serif look (it still feels collegiate and trustworthy), but prices, product descriptions, buttons, and nav links switched to a cleaner, modern sans-serif.

**Why it helps:** Prices and product details are now easier to read at a glance, which matters most right at the moment someone is deciding whether to buy — if the price or stock info is hard to read, that's friction that can lose a sale. This is also just how real retail sites do it (both reference stores use a clean sans-serif for anything functional), so the site now reads as more like a real store and less like a school report.

## 2. Used the Yale-blue color more consistently

The site had two different dark blues doing similar jobs. Now one of them — the official-feeling Yale blue — is used consistently for anything "brand," like the top nav bar, the chat window, and the selected size button, while the other blue is reserved just for text.

**Why it helps:** A single, consistent brand color makes the site feel more deliberately designed and more official, which builds a little more trust — and trust is a big factor in whether someone is comfortable entering their info and buying from a site they've never used before.

## 3. Added a "Shop by Category" section to the homepage

The homepage used to go straight from the welcome banner into a random small sample of 4 products. Now there's a row of clickable category tiles (T-Shirts, Hoodies, Crewnecks, etc.) right under the banner, each showing a real count of how many items are in that category, that jump straight to a filtered product list.

**Why it helps:** Someone landing on the homepage usually already has something specific in mind ("I want a hoodie"). Before, they'd have to click into Products and then search or scroll through all 102 items themselves. Now they can go from the homepage to exactly what they're looking for in one click. Fewer clicks and less hunting means fewer people giving up and leaving before they ever see something they'd want to buy.

## 4. Added real "only X left" stock badges on product cards

Every product card (on the homepage, the Products page, chat results, and recommendations) now shows a small badge when that item is actually low in stock or sold out — and the number shown is always the real, current count from the database, never made up.

**Why it helps:** This is the single change most likely to directly increase sales. Seeing that something is genuinely running low creates a real, honest sense of urgency — people are more likely to decide "I should get this now" instead of "I'll think about it later" (and then forget). Because the number is always pulled from the real inventory, it's a trustworthy nudge, not a fake marketing trick.

## 5. Added a "You Might Also Like" section to each product page

Each individual product page now ends with a row of genuinely similar products — same type of item, or sharing real tags like color/team/style — instead of just stopping after the description.

**Why it helps:** Before, a customer who clicked into one product and decided it wasn't quite right had nowhere else to go but to click all the way back to the full list. Now there's always an obvious next option right there, which keeps people browsing instead of leaving the site, and gives them more chances to find something they actually want to buy.

## 6. Gave the chat assistant a name: "Ask the Bulldog"

The chat button and chat window used to say generic things like "Chat" and "Campus Customs Assistant." It now says "Ask the Bulldog" everywhere.

**Why it helps:** A named, personality-driven assistant feels more like a friendly helper and less like a generic customer-service bot, which makes people more likely to actually click it and ask a question. Every question answered through chat is a chance to point someone toward a product they'll buy, so getting more people to actually use the chat is directly good for sales.
